# FlowCFO editorial standards

Use this skill for article ideas, drafts, rewrites, comparisons, SEO copy, and admin blog-generation prompts.

## Audience and voice

- Write for a global audience by default. FlowCFO is not a country-specific brand. Do not make Malaysia, the United States, or any other market the presumed reader.
- Use clear international English, practical examples, and neutral “currency units” when the illustration does not need a jurisdiction. Explain any regional legal, regulatory, tax, or price example as explicitly local.
- Author: Joshua O. Editorial voice: informed, humane, specific, candid about uncertainty, useful rather than promotional. Define financial terms before relying on them.
- Typical substantial article: 800–1,400 words; do not pad. A short answer can be shorter only when the topic genuinely warrants it. Structure for scanning with meaningful H2/H3s, examples, tables/checklists where they help, and a clear takeaway.
- Every article should teach a reader something actionable, show limitations and common mistakes, and end with an appropriate next step. Add a concise FlowCFO CTA only when product fit is relevant; never imply that FlowCFO guarantees an outcome.

## Research and sources

- Browse for claims that may change, niche facts, regulation, competitor capabilities, prices, and product availability. Prefer primary sources: official product/pricing pages, standards bodies, regulators, and research.
- Link citations close to the claim and include a source list. Name source, geography, and checked date for time-sensitive facts.
- Do not imply a source is universal when it is issued by one country’s agency. Do not cite outdated archived sources as current policy. Avoid unsupported statistics, invented quotes, unattributed claims, and fabricated case studies.
- Never quietly use one country’s examples across a globally framed article. If a local example adds value, label it in the copy and diversify other references where suitable.

## Article types that serve search intent

- Explainers: clarify an enduring concept (cash vs profit, liquidity, statement reading) with a worked example.
- Practical guides/DIY: give sequenced steps, templates, decision checks, caveats, and what to do when a step fails.
- Comparisons/buying guides: identify the reader and decision first; compare real products on job-to-be-done, features, limitations, integrations, eligibility, support, privacy/deployment, and pricing. Link official home/pricing/product pages. State the country and date for pricing, monthly versus annual rates, currency, taxes, trial/promotional status, and quote-only pricing. Explain who should choose each option and where FlowCFO differs. Do not manufacture parity or call FlowCFO a replacement when it is not.
- Case studies: use only permissioned, verifiable customer evidence. Clearly label synthetic or illustrative examples.
- Glossary/FAQ/checklists: answer a distinct query concisely, link deeper material, avoid doorway-page repetition.

## FlowCFO claim guardrails

- Distinguish current demonstrable behavior, customer-configured work, proposed/pilot work, and roadmap. Do not turn a prospect’s requested feature into an existing capability.
- Describe model inference separately from training. A customer-provided model key or endpoint does not mean that customer data trains a foundation model.
- Do not claim regulated advice, lending eligibility, guaranteed delinquency prevention, bank connectivity, certification, or enterprise readiness unless evidence and approval exist.
- State that educational articles are not individualized financial, investment, accounting, tax, or legal advice.

## Editorial QA

Check factual claims, product names, source links, jurisdiction labels, spelling, reading flow, CTA relevance, title/description, dates, author, and article schema fields before publication. Do not create backdated publication metadata to misrepresent when an article was actually published; use a truthful publication date and a separate updated date when supported.
